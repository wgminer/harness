Onboarding Sprint

How should Harness open for someone with no AI keys? The team's answer: lead with on-device dictation, get to a first spoken sentence before asking for anything else, and make chat an upgrade the user chooses.

1

Map

Long-term goal

Someone Will hands Harness to is dictating with it daily within their first day, without ever needing an API key. They add a key or local model only when they want more.

Today's first run

A "Welcome to Harness" modal asks for an OpenAI key. "Skip for now" dismisses it for good, and chat then shows a disabled compose box that points at Settings > Accounts. Dictation already works on-device, but nothing in the first run says so through action.

What the experts agreed on

  • Dictation is the wedge: no key, account, or card.
  • The real drop-off is the three macOS permissions, mainly Accessibility.
  • A greyed-out chat box reads as a broken app.
  • Local models handle polish and titles well and agentic chat badly.
Decider calls

Target: a Mac person Will knows, not an AI tinkerer, from launch to their first fn dictation into another app.

Local scope: dictation plus local polish and titles (Apple Foundation Models where available, else a small optional download). Chat stays key-gated, without any dead UI.

Supervote

E as the base, with B's "Open Notes for me" and C's toast when fn is pressed in another app before the grant.

Polish appears in the practice pad before Accessibility, so the hard permission comes after a win. After onboarding, chat shows a sample conversation with costs in plain money terms. The practice pad stays throwaway; nothing is saved as a note.

Debrief

Decision

The first half is validated. Opening on dictation, with each permission explained just before macOS asks, got all five participants through the microphone and speech prompts. The Accessibility step is still untested, and every participant was already wary of it before reaching it. Build the first half now and test Accessibility with real people before building it.

Copy and layout changes from the test

  • Show two steps at first, not three. Reveal "Type in other apps" after the first dictation, along with its reason.
  • Rename "Speech" to "Voice to text." Before the macOS prompt, say plainly that recognition runs on the Mac and nothing is sent to Apple, once that's verified.
  • Turn the footer into a link that shows how audio stays on the Mac. All five wanted proof.
  • Collapse the sidebar to Home until the first dictation, then let the rest appear.
  • Pulse the key twice, then stop. Draw fn or the globe key to match the keyboard.

Next slices

1
Verify on-device speech. Every promise in this flow depends on it. Desktop Apple Speech runs through the helper's --engine apple; confirm it requires on-device recognition, or force it.
2
Dictation-first first run. Replace the key modal with the practice pad, add in-context Mic and Speech asks with live status and denial recovery, and use the copy above. Roughly one slice: it reuses the existing setup gaps and the mic permission code.
3
Honest chat without a key. A sample conversation, "billed per use, separate from ChatGPT Plus," and Add a key. This removes the disabled compose box.
4
Accessibility step. Test it on two or three real people first. Then build the live wait, relaunch-and-resume, the "inside Harness only" path, and the toast when fn is pressed elsewhere.
5
Local polish and titles. Apple Foundation Models behind the genai seam, with a diff guard. Offer a one-time download on Macs without it. About three slices.

The participants and the team were simulated. Treat these findings as strong hypotheses. Check the Accessibility step, trust in the on-device claim, and Apple Speech accuracy on names and accents with real people before investing in slices 4 and 5.
